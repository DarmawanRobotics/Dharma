\chapter{Simulation}
\label{ch:sim}

\pkg{dharma_sim} is a software-in-the-loop robot: one node, \code{sim_robot_node}, replaces the
robot driver, the Livox Mid-360, its IMU and the LiDAR-inertial estimator, so the whole stack
runs without hardware, in CI and on a laptop. It is kinematic (no Gazebo, no GPU). A product runs
its own bringup against it with \code{odometry method:=external} and drives it like the real
robot; its end-to-end tests run the same way.

\section{Why the simulator models errors}

The 0.1 simulator had perfect odometry and stamped everything with \code{now()} (finding S1). The
code paths that handle drift (localization corrections, L1), map smearing (M1), a tilted start
(O1) and stale TF (T1, T3) were therefore never exercised. Version 0.2.0 models them, with a seed
so every run is reproducible.

\section{Models}

All models are pure Python and numpy in \code{dharma_sim/core} and unit-tested without ROS;
\code{simulation.py} runs them on one clock.

\begin{description}
  \item[Body] (\code{body.py}). Omnidirectional or differential from \param{robot.kinematics}
    (a \code{diff} robot never strafes), with the acceleration limits of the profile. It
    integrates the \emph{measured} time step as an exact arc, clamped to \param{max_dt} (0.1 used a
    fixed step per wall-timer tick, so the simulated robot slowed down under load, review \#14).
    A command older than \param{cmd_timeout_sec} brakes the body to a stop, like the driver.
    A contact of the footprint with the world stops the body and latches \topic{sim/collision}
    (an obstacle dropped onto the robot counts as a contact).
  \item[World] (\code{world.py}). Walls (polylines with a height) and circles (pillars, people,
    boxes) with a height, from a YAML world in \code{config/worlds/}; dynamic circles from the
    scenario or from \topic{sim/obstacles}. Ray casting is correct for origins inside a circle
    (0.1 used $t = -b - \sqrt{\mathrm{disc}}$ there and was blind, review \#10), for tangent rays
    and for circles behind the origin.
  \item[LiDAR] (\code{lidar.py}). A Mid-360: \param{lidar.rings} elevations over the vertical
    field of view from $-7^\circ$ to $52^\circ$ at the mount height (an obstacle lower than the
    lowest beam is not seen, as on the robot), \param{azimuth_rays} rays per ring, 0.1--40~m,
    floor returns of the downward rings, Gaussian range noise and random point dropout from the
    seeded RNG. The cloud is dense enough for perception (well above \param{blind.min_points} in
    the height band of the mall world).
  \item[Odometry drift] (\code{odometry.py}). Every true step $\Delta\vect{T}$ of the body is
    measured as
    \begin{equation}
      \vect{\xi} = \big((1+s)\,\Delta x,\ (1+s)\,\Delta y,\ \Delta\psi + b\,\Delta d\big) +
      \vect{n}, \quad \vect{n} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vect{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,\Delta s), \qquad
      \T{odom}{base} \leftarrow \T{odom}{base} \oplus \vect{\xi},
    \end{equation}
    with the scale error $s$, the yaw bias $b$ per metre, the distance $\Delta d$ and the travel
    $\Delta s = \Delta d + w_\psi |\Delta\psi|$ (\param{rotation_weight} $w_\psi$, so turning in
    place drifts too). The variance grows linearly with the distance (a random walk, standard
    deviation $\propto \sqrt{d}$) and the biases accumulate with it. An odometry restart starts a
    new origin at the current pose, like a restarted estimator.
  \item[Latency] (\code{latency.py}). Sensor messages carry $\tau = t - L$ and show the state of
    that time (an interpolated pose history), so a late stamp is late data, not only a late
    label; commands act $L$ after they arrive and become zero once stale. A clock jump back
    clears the command queue.
  \item[IMU] (\code{imu.py}). Specific force in g and yaw rate on \topic{/livox/imu}, so the
    bridge levels at once. The IMU sits at $(0.011, 0.02329, -0.04412)$~m in \frm{livox_frame}
    (Livox manual); FAST-LIO's \code{extrinsic_T} is the LiDAR in the IMU frame, its negative.
    0.1 used FAST-LIO's $T_{IL}$ with the wrong sign (review \#11).
\end{description}

The body is planar: no gait, pitch or roll, no ceiling, no negative obstacles. Experiments that
depend on body motion (the pitched floor of H13) or on the real estimator therefore need
recorded data or the robot.

With the localization bridge (\frm{odom}$\to$\frm{camera_init}, \frm{body}$\to$\frm{base_link})
this gives \frm{odom}$\to$\frm{base_link}. Perception publishes \code{BLOCKED} scans unless it
gets that chain; the mux forwards nothing without a fresh, stamped, 360° safety scan in
\frm{base_link}. The simulator publishes no \topic{/livox/lidar} (the raw driver topic): the hub's
\code{robot_bringup/config/sim.yaml} points health at \topic{/cloud_registered_body} instead.

\section{Scenarios and site fixture}

Scenarios are YAML files in \code{config/scenarios/} (times in seconds since start), loaded by
the node (\param{scenario_file}, a path or a name) and by tests. A scenario may set the seed
(it replaces the \param{seed} parameter, so the same file gives the same run), override the
latency and odometry noise parameters, add circles that appear, move with $(v_x, v_y)$ and leave,
silence sensors (\code{lidar}, \code{odometry}, \code{imu} or \code{all}) for a duration, and
restart the odometry at given times. Independent generators are derived from the seed for the
LiDAR, the odometry and the IMU (\code{numpy.random.SeedSequence}), so changing one model does
not change the others' noise.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lcX@{}}
  \toprule
  Scenario & Seed & Content \\
  \midrule
  \code{drift} & 1 & $\sigma_{x,y} = 0.01$~m$/\sqrt{\mathrm{m}}$, $\sigma_\psi = 0.3^\circ/\sqrt{\mathrm{m}}$,
    1\,\% scale error, $0.1^\circ$/m yaw bias: localization corrections (L1) \\
  \code{person_crossing} & 2 & a person crosses the south corridor (8--30~s), a 0.5~m box stays
    on the route from 20~s: avoidance, protective field, replanning \\
  \code{sensor_faults} & 3 & latency 0.15~s LiDAR, 0.05~s odometry and commands; LiDAR (1.5~s),
    odometry (0.8~s) and full (1~s) dropouts; an odometry restart at 40~s \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The site fixture (\code{site_fixture.py}) rasterizes a world into a loadable site:
\code{map.pgm} and \code{map.yaml} (map\_server trinary, with size and CRC), \code{tags.json} with
the world's tag plates and \code{routes/*.csv} (\code{dharma_utils.io}). The launch argument
\code{site:=mall_loop} draws it into \param{sites_dir} (default \code{/tmp/dharma_sim/sites})
when the site has no map yet, including the taught loop \code{config/routes/mall_loop.csv}
with one stop point; \pkg{dharma_mapping}'s site store reads it unchanged.

\section{Launch}

\begin{lstlisting}
ros2 launch dharma_sim sim.launch.py robot_params:=robot.yaml site:=mall_loop
ros2 launch dharma_sim sim.launch.py scenario:=person_crossing rviz:=true
ros2 topic pub --once /sim/obstacles geometry_msgs/msg/PoseArray \
  "{poses: [{position: {x: 2.0, y: -3.0}}]}"
\end{lstlisting}

Arguments: \code{params_file}, \code{robot_params}, \code{use_sim_time}, \code{log_level} (the
launch contract of Section~\ref{sec:launch}), \code{world}, \code{scenario}, \code{robot_ns}
(empty: from the parameter files), \code{site}, \code{sites_dir} and \code{rviz} (the
\pkg{dharma_rviz} layout with the driver topics remapped).

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:eval-protocol}

The experiments of the paper run on the mall world with these models: E1 stopping distance
against $D(v)$, E2 blind LiDAR in AUTO and REMOTE, E3 localization error with and without the EKF
and scan matching under seeded odometry drift (the \code{drift} model), E4 path shift clearance
and completion time with standing and crossing people (the \code{person_crossing} pattern).
Every run is defined by a scenario file and its seed, so a result can be reproduced exactly and a
regression shows in CI. The measured results, the number of seeds and runs per condition and
the scripts that produce them are reported with the paper's evaluation (Section~VI of the paper
in \code{docs/paper}), not duplicated here.
